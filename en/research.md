# Research and publications

My doctoral research investigates how humans, artificial intelligence agents, and heterogeneous robots can acquire, verify, transfer, and improve physical skills over time.

## Research line

### Multi-agent cumulative culture for physical skills

The proposal integrates extended reality, artificial intelligence, and robotics so that a skill taught by a person can retain its provenance, adapt to different robots, and evolve without losing verifiable safety and performance constraints.

The proposed system integrates:

- Collaborative robots.
- Meta Quest 3 and Microsoft HoloLens 2.
- Digital twins and spatial tracking.
- Gesture and pattern recognition.
- Predictive models of human behavior.
- Neuro-symbolic artificial intelligence.
- Reinforcement learning and multi-agent systems.
- Skill genealogy and transfer.
- Verifiable invariants and safety constraints.
- Real-time communication.

## Research problem

Programming and operating industrial robots requires specialized technical knowledge. This hinders adoption in activities where users need to teach motions or collaborate directly with a robot.

Mixed reality interfaces can reduce this difficulty by presenting instructions, warnings, trajectories, and robot states within the user's workspace.

## General objective

Develop and evaluate a multi-agent architecture that enables physical skills to be learned, transferred, and improved between humans and heterogeneous robots through extended reality, while preserving traceability and verifiable constraints.

## Specific objectives

1. Design a mixed reality interface for interacting with a collaborative robot.
2. Implement a digital twin connected to the robot.
3. Record demonstrations, motions, and decisions from expert users.
4. Represent skills in a form transferable across heterogeneous robots.
5. Maintain a genealogy documenting the origin and evolution of each skill.
6. Incorporate intelligent agents, learning, and verifiable constraints.
7. Evaluate transfer, accuracy, safety, time, and ease of learning.

## Research questions

- Can mixed reality reduce the time required to learn how to operate a collaborative robot?
- How can a community of humans and robots accumulate reusable physical knowledge?
- Which components of a skill remain invariant when the robot changes?
- How can the safety of a learned or modified skill be verified?
- What information should the interface display to improve safety without increasing cognitive load?
- How can symbolic knowledge and machine learning be combined to assist the operator?
- What differences emerge between interaction through controllers, hands, and virtual objects?

## General methodology

1. Mixed reality system development.
2. Digital twin integration.
3. Collaborative robot connection.
4. Experimental task design.
5. Participant studies.
6. Recording of motions, errors, and completion times.
7. Quantitative and qualitative analysis.
8. Safety and usability evaluation.
